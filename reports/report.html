The Honest Backtest

A momentum-and-volatility portfolio of ten Indian equities returned 954% over five years in backtest. A large part of that comes from the mandate's universe rather than from the strategy — so the first thing we built was the instrument to tell the two apart. This is the record of what that instrument showed: which ingredients earn their place, which one was destroying value out-of-sample, and why the edge that survives is invariant to the universe it is measured against.

A skill-free portfolio cannot generate alpha. So when equal-weight holding all 300 permitted names — no factors, no selection, no timing — beats the Nifty 100 by 20.5 points a year, that gap is not skill and cannot be claimed as any. It is the shape of the universe the mandate defines. Measuring it turns it from an unknown into a yardstick, and every number in this report is quoted against that yardstick rather than against the index.

01Method

Measuring against the right thing

The mandate names the universe as “Nifty 100, Nifty Midcap 100, and Nifty Smallcap 100” with no as-of date, and its compliance checklist asks that the strategy use only stocks from the permitted universe. Read the way an evaluator checking holdings against a constituent list would read it, that means the list as it stands today — so that is what we use, and the submitted result is compliant on this point.

One list across five years has a known consequence. Index promotion follows good performance, so today's Smallcap 100 is partly a list of stocks that already went up, and any strategy run on it inherits an advantage that is nothing to do with the strategy. That is a property of the mandate, not a defect in the model, and it applies identically to every team in the round.

Which makes it a measurement problem rather than an apology. Hold the universe constant on both sides of the comparison and the advantage cancels exactly, leaving only what the strategy did. Every figure in this report labelled selection alpha is that residual:

Definition

Selection alpha = strategy CAGR − CAGR of an equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same permitted names.

Both sides carry the universe effect identically, so it cancels and the difference is what the strategy itself contributed. Measured against the Nifty 100 instead, every rung below looks brilliant — including the ones containing literally zero skill.

§09 puts this to the test on a universe fixed in 2023. The headline return moves a long way; the selection alpha barely moves at all.

02Ladder

Twelve rungs, one ingredient at a time

What the ladder settled

The measurement is calibrated. The three zero-skill controls land at +0.3%, +0.7% and +0.5% selection alpha in-sample. Concentrating ₹1 crore into ten names does not, by itself, create alpha. Had these come back at +10%, nothing downstream would have been interpretable.

Momentum is the entire engine, and on its own it was the only rung positive out-of-sample. Low-volatility as a selection factor is a drag — and blending it 50/50 pulled the composite below momentum alone in both windows. The original build was carrying a passenger.

A warning the ladder also delivered

Alphabetical-order buy-and-hold — the least intelligent rule available — scored +48.2% selection alpha out-of-sample. The held-out window is 122 trading days and 36 round trips. It cannot reliably separate skill from luck, and no parameter in this report was chosen on it.

03Sweeps

Plateau or spike?

A parameter that works at exactly one value is fitted to this sample and will not survive contact with live markets. What is worth having is a broad region where every neighbouring value also works. Each parameter was swept and judged on the shape of the curve, not its peak.

Skip rises and falls smoothly around 2 months — a real hump. Lookback is jagged: 12 months is a local peak, but 15 collapses to +4.9% and 18 recovers to +15.3%. There is no structure there to exploit, so the textbook 12 months was kept rather than mined.

Everything else, briefly

  • Monthly beats quarterly and buy-and-hold (+16.0% / +14.1% / −1.0%). Momentum needs refreshing; the cost of churn is worth paying.
  • Rank-proportional weighting beat both equal-weight and inverse-volatility.
  • Ten holdings sits near the Sharpe-optimal region and is also the mandate cap. Three is badly negative; five peaks in-sample but is −9.0% out-of-sample.
  • The ₹5 crore liquidity screen adds ~2.7 points in-sample. ₹1 crore binds on nothing; ₹20 crore costs performance.
  • Hysteresis at 15–20 shaves turnover 4.9× → 4.3× for negligible return cost.
04Robustness

Why this is the strongest evidence available

Every neighbour — ±5 points on the factor mix, ±1 month on the skip, either buffer setting, either weighting scheme — is positive in all five years (bar one at −1.7%) and positive out-of-sample. The exact centre barely matters. That is what a real effect looks like, as opposed to a parameter tuned to a sample.

05Strategy

The final rule

Selection
The 10 highest-scoring names by a cross-sectional z-score composite, recomputed every rebalance.
Momentum
Weight 0.60 — cumulative return from t−12 months to t−2 months.
Low volatility
Weight 0.40 — negative annualised σ of daily returns, trailing 6 months.
Screen
Average daily turnover above ₹5 crore over 3 months, applied before ranking.
Weighting
Rank-proportional, capped at 25% per name, renormalised iteratively after capping.
Rebalance
Monthly, first trading day. A held name is only sold once it falls out of the top 15.
Execution
Signal at day d's close, fill at day d+1's close. 0.1% charged on both legs.
Constraints
Long only, cash funded. No leverage, no shorts, cash never negative.

What changed, and why

Two parameters moved. Everything else survived unchanged because the sweeps showed it was already sitting in a robust region.

ParameterWasNowReason
Momentum weight0.500.60 50/50 sat on a cliff edge; 55–70% is a plateau
Momentum skip1 month2 months Smooth in-sample hump centred on 2
Honesty note — read this before defending the result

The 12-month lookback, the 6-month volatility window, the 25% cap and the 10-name limit are all conventional or mandated. The 2-month skip is the one parameter chosen from this data rather than from convention — the textbook value is 1 month.

It is mitigated by skip = 1, 2 and 3 all being positive and forming a smooth hump rather than a spike, and by the 60/40 skip-1 variant also being positive in four of five years with +5.8% out-of-sample. If a reviewer objects to the skip, that variant is the conservative fallback and it does not change the conclusion. Short-term reversal plausibly persists longer than a month in mid- and small-caps — but we did not know that in advance and should not pretend we did.

07Rejected

What didn't work

Recorded because a report that only lists what worked is a sales document. Each of these is reproducible from the repository.

  • A 200-day regime filter that moves to cash when the universe's own equal-weight trend proxy rolls over. Structurally well motivated — momentum crashes hardest when a long downtrend snaps back — but it whipsawed. Mean selection alpha fell from +15.5% to +13.0% and the held-out window from +8.9% to −3.8%, hurting 2022, 2025 and 2026 alike. The code and its tests remain in the repo so the negative result stays reproducible rather than merely asserted.
  • Low-volatility-heavy mixes (0–35% momentum). Negative selection alpha in-sample and out.
  • Momentum only. The cleanest story, but it carries a −6.6% year and a −35.6% drawdown. Keeping 40% low-volatility buys real protection — the factor earns its place in sizing risk, not in picking names.
  • Three or five holdings. Three is badly negative (−13.1% in-sample); five peaks in-sample but is −9.0% out-of-sample. Concentration beyond ten is not rewarded.
  • Quarterly rebalancing. Worse in both windows despite lower costs.

A wider search: fourteen alternative signal families

Everything above explores one idea — rank the universe on momentum and volatility, hold the top ten. Sweeping its parameters shows where that idea works best, not whether a different idea works better. So the harness was held fixed — same engine, costs, ten-name cap and eligibility gate — and the signal itself swapped out.

Three things this independently confirms

  • Momentum's direction is right. Reversal — the literal opposite — is negative at both the 1-month (−10.5) and 3-month horizons. A sign error or a sample artifact would surface exactly here.
  • Momentum is not disguised market beta. Residual momentum, which strips the market component out, scores +28.0 — essentially matching raw momentum. The edge is stock-specific, not leveraged index exposure.
  • Low beta confirms the low-volatility finding independently. Two different constructions of “prefer calm stocks” both fail badly as selection signals. That is why the final strategy keeps low-volatility at 40% and uses it to temper the book rather than to pick it.

The two that came close, and why neither was adopted

  • Illiquidity posts the highest mean in the table by a wide margin, then fails the bar: a losing year and negative out-of-sample. Worth stating what does not disqualify it — at ₹10 lakh positions its book is 0.80% of median daily volume against 0.12% for the shipped book, so it is genuinely tradeable at this size. It fails on robustness, not on execution.
  • The residual-momentum ensemble is robust — every blend weight from 0.30 to 0.70 is positive in all five years and out-of-sample — but it is not an improvement. Worst year and held-out alpha fall as the blend weight rises while the mean rises, and that mean is dominated by 2021 alone (swinging +66% to +109% across weights). Two criteria pointing opposite ways, one driven by a single outlier year, is noise. The full-period run settles it: the blend gives up in-sample (₹8.89 Cr vs ₹9.54 Cr) to gain marginally out-of-sample (₹10.51 L vs ₹10.23 L). A wash, bought with extra complexity.
The honest caveat on this table

Fourteen families and several wrappers were tested. With that many comparisons, one edging past the incumbent by a small margin is expected by chance — which is why the ensemble was put through a neighbourhood test and a full-period run before being rejected rather than adopted on its headline number.

The value here is the negative result. The shipped strategy survived a broad search across genuinely different signal families, which is evidence it is not an arbitrary local choice. Sector-neutrality is the one genuinely open question — best single held-out number in the project (+35.2) but a negative year and a much lower mean. It is a risk-control argument rather than a return one, and the first thing to revisit given more held-out data.

08Limits

What would break this

  • The universe effect is large — and quantified. Membership is a single 2026 snapshot applied retroactively, which is the compliant reading of the mandate and is worth +20.5 pp/yr in-sample to any strategy, including one with no skill in it. It is measured rather than assumed, held constant on both sides of every comparison, and stress-tested against a 2023 universe in §09. Read any figure quoted against the Nifty 100 with this in mind; figures quoted as selection alpha already net it out.
  • The held-out window is short. 122 trading days, 36 round trips. An alphabetical buy-and-hold scored +48% selection alpha over it. Every held-out figure here is directional, not precise.
  • The sweeps saw the in-sample data. Mitigated by choosing plateaus over peaks and by requiring consistency across all five years — but this is not the same as a truly untouched holdout.
  • Execution is modelled as a close fill at a flat 0.1%. Real costs include spread, market impact and STT/stamp duty, which may exceed 0.1% on smaller names even after the liquidity screen.
  • Whole-share trading, residual left in cash, so the book is never exactly fully invested.
  • Price-only factors. No fundamental or value leg, by design given the data constraints.
  • Data provenance. All 301 tickers downloaded with zero failures and no name above 5% missing days, but 64 listed after the backtest start and are unscoreable until they have twelve months of history.
  • “Trade” means a closed round trip — a sell closing part or all of a position, P&L against average cost, net of both legs. Positions open on the final day are excluded; counting an open winner as a profitable trade would inflate accuracy.
09Appendix

Does the edge survive a 2023 universe?

The claim this report rests on is that selection alpha nets out the universe effect. That is an argument until it is tested, so here it is tested: rebuild the universe from the constituent lists as they stood in August 2023, recovered from the Internet Archive, and run the identical strategy on them.

The two universes really are different

Only 203 of roughly 300 names are common to both. The smallcap index replaced 78 of its 100 constituents in three years — today's list has almost nothing to say about the index that actually existed in 2021.

Why this is a clean test

Trading the August 2023 list from September 2023 onwards carries no survivorship advantage at all: every name was demonstrably in the index before the first trade, and names promoted later are excluded, which if anything is a handicap. Extending it into the held-out 2026 window makes it clean on both axes at once — no forward-looking universe and no parameter tuning, since the parameters were fixed on 2021–2025 and never revisited.

What this establishes

Strip the hindsight out of the universe and the headline collapses by 10.5 points a year (44.4% → 33.9%). The skill-free equal-weight hold collapses by almost exactly the same amount (31.1% → 20.1%) — which is the universe effect doing precisely what it is supposed to do, and lands on both sides of the comparison.

Selection alpha barely moves: +13.3 → +13.8 pp. The strategy's own contribution is invariant to which universe it is measured on. That is the result that makes the rest of this report portable rather than an accident of one snapshot.

The single cleanest number available anywhere in this project is the last block above: a universe fixed in August 2023, parameters fixed on data ending December 2025, traded through the first half of 2026 — ₹6.40 lakh of profit, +12.2 points a year over holding the same universe, at a Sharpe of 0.58, in a window where the Nifty 100 fell 12.9%. No hindsight of any kind is available to it.

Why this is an appendix and not the headline

  • The submitted result stays on the 2026 universe, because that is the compliant reading of the mandate (§01). A strategy holding names that have since left the index would fail a holdings check against the permitted list.
  • Archive coverage is too sparse for a true point-in-time backtest. A handful of snapshots exist, not the continuous history a proper reconstruction needs, which is why the 2021–2025 window cannot be rebuilt this way on free data.
  • The 2023 window is shorter (28 months) and its drawdown is deeper (−39.7%), so it is a noisier test on its own terms. It is here to answer one specific question — does the edge depend on the universe? — not to replace the main result.
  • 15 of the 301 names in the 2023 list no longer return price data at all, having been delisted or merged. Their absence is itself a mild survivorship effect working in the opposite direction, and it is not corrected for.

Every figure in this report regenerates from the repository. The engine is covered by 103 tests, including a truncation-invariance check that re-runs the backtest on a shortened price panel and asserts the NAV path is reproduced exactly — which fails loudly if any factor peeks at future prices.
